## What the law and frameworks require or recommend

**AI hypothesis — Rule candidate (L1).** Sections 14–15 impose duties on the
Statistician; s 15's engagement principles address the engagements required by
ss 19, 35 and 91. They are not worded as a general private-tool consultation
licence. For Stats NZ research access, ss 48–51 require consideration of public
benefit, Māori wellbeing and harm, cultural competence, relevant connections,
and data sensitivity/security. Section 48 permits conditions on use and
publication and preserves supplier access conditions. Section 54 binds an
authorised researcher to the approved research and conditions, protection
against identification, and publication/disclosure of results and methods,
subject to its exceptions. These conditions would matter even for nongovernment
researchers. [L1, ss 14–15, 48–54](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2022/39/en/2026-06-03/).

**AI hypothesis — Rule candidate (L2).** Sections 4 and 8 include New Zealand
private agencies and ordinarily resident individuals; government ownership is
not the test. Section 7 defines personal information through an identifiable
individual. Section 22 requires a lawful necessary collection purpose,
reasonable notices, safeguards, access/correction, accuracy, justified retention,
and permitted use/disclosure; IPP 12 governs qualifying overseas disclosures.
Sections 114–115 address notifiable breaches and s 201 privacy officers.
IPP 3A adds indirect-collection notification subject to its exceptions and
s 25A's 1 May 2026 boundary. Statistical/research exceptions in IPPs 2, 3/3A,
10 and 11 are conditional; they are not a waiver of all principles.
[L2, cited sections](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2020/31/en/2026-09-23/).

**AI hypothesis — Fact candidate (M1).** The 2018 principles ask for Māori
control throughout the data lifecycle, metadata, collective benefit and
accountability to originating communities. Principles 3.1, 5.2 and 6.3 address
collective versus individual rights, free prior informed consent with stronger
governance where consent is less defined, and Māori decisions about restricted
or open access. Principle 2.2 values disaggregation that serves Māori priorities.
These are the network's articulated expectations, not a retrieved statutory
permission to release prohibited Poltika outputs. [M1, p 2](https://www.temanararaunga.maori.nz/s/TMR-Maori-Data-Sovereignty-Principles-Oct-2018.pdf).

**AI hypothesis — Fact candidate (M2–M3).** Ngā Tikanga Paihere expects
relationships before substantive research, involvement early in decisions,
accountability, relevant expertise, provenance and attention to group harms.
The guidelines encourage application beyond IDI. For Data Lab applications,
Stats NZ assesses evidence of support and cultural responsiveness; its
application guidance identifies authority, expertise or lived experience as
relevant to suitable representation. [M2, pp 3, 7, 10–16](https://data.govt.nz/assets/data-ethics/Nga-Tikanga/Nga-Tikanga-Paihere-Guidelines-December-2020.pdf),
[M3, relationship/accountability headings](https://www.stats.govt.nz/integrated-data/apply-to-use-microdata-for-research/how-to-apply-nga-tikanga-paihere-to-microdata-research-projects/).

**AI hypothesis — Fact candidate (M6–M9).** M6 asks agencies to address
collective privacy and negotiate consent over time; pp 44–45 explicitly discuss
tension with statistical/research exceptions. M7's scope includes deterministic
algorithmic systems as well as AI. M8 extends practical guidance to businesses.
M9 asks government agencies to carry protection expectations into provider
agreements, including storage and culturally appropriate aggregation. The
sources distinguish statutory authority, ethical expectations and contractual
controls; none retrieves a Poltika-specific agreement.
[M6, pp 33–37, 43–45](https://www.kahuiraraunga.io/_files/ugd/b8e45c_a5b7af8b688c4cd9b7583775c27da52e.pdf),
[M7, pp 6–7](https://www.kahuiraraunga.io/_files/ugd/b8e45c_79f4162207da4123861c50dec08cd0fb.pdf),
[M8, practical guidance](https://data.govt.nz/leadership/centre-for-data-ethics-and-innovation/guidance/maori-data-and-ai-guidance-for-business),
[M9, third-party expectations](https://www.digital.govt.nz/standards-and-guidance/information-sharing-standard/maori-data).

## Unresolved readings and verification limits

The following are **AI hypotheses**, not resolved interpretations.

- **Consent and research authority:** L2's conditional statistical/research
  exceptions and L1's access authorisation may permit some processing without
  individual consent. M1/M6 call for collective governance and consent, with
  explicit justification where consent differs. “Legally accessible” and
  “governance agreed” answer different questions. The sources do not settle
  Poltika's consent/governance arrangement; escalate the proposed dataset and
  use before either is presumed sufficient.
- **Which rights holders and representatives:** M3's contextual relationships
  can include several roles; M4 preserves iwi/member autonomy and other
  relationships. A pan-Māori mechanism, project-specific community governance,
  or both are possible readings for different scopes. This document chooses
  none and names no person for engagement.
- **Private-tool adaptation:** M6/M7 primarily address the public service;
  M8 addresses businesses and M9 government providers. Voluntary adoption,
  contractual adoption and directly applicable law are distinct. No retrieved
  passage makes every public-service directive a direct statutory duty for an
  independent calculator.
- **Version and agreement gaps:** M2's imprint/filename differ; M7 has a year
  but no verified exact release date. No later replacement was retrieved from
  the owners, but unpublished replacement status is unverified. Current
  individual iwi agreement texts, amendments, termination status and any
  Poltika-relevant data conditions remain unverified. Do not fill those gaps
  from memory or a reported relationship count.
