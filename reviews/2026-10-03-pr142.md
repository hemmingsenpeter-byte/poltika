## Tooling choice and limits

Use [Bandit 1.9.4](https://github.com/PyCQA/bandit/releases/tag/1.9.4)
([Apache-2.0](https://github.com/PyCQA/bandit/blob/1.9.4/LICENSE)) and
[zizmor 1.30.1](https://github.com/zizmorcore/zizmor/releases/tag/v1.30.1)
([MIT](https://github.com/zizmorcore/zizmor/blob/v1.30.1/LICENSE)).
CodeQL's [action terms](https://github.com/github/codeql-action/blob/2892aa5e19bbd11bc0cff5427e3b750a04d9e3c2/README.md#license)
and [CLI terms](https://github.com/github/codeql-cli-binaries/blob/main/LICENSE.md)
do not make disabled uploads an entitlement. The repository remains private;
no licence purchase or publication decision is made.

Bandit scans all production Python and retains every severity. The initial
baseline fails on scanner errors or high-severity findings. Four medium B314
XML-parser findings in `src/poltika/data/xlsx.py` and 35 low findings are printed
and retained for engineering triage; this PR neither suppresses nor resolves
them. Tightening that threshold after reviewed remediation is a later bounded
change. zizmor fails on every reported workflow issue. Reverting this scanner
choice requires a reviewed tooling PR and, for private CodeQL use, verified
entitlement. These scanners have different coverage from CodeQL.

Action SHAs were resolved from the official repositories' existing version
tags on this date, retaining the intended action versions. Both guards already
fetch full history in checkout; retained-code replay uses local commits/worktrees.
Checkout credential persistence is unnecessary for those operations.
